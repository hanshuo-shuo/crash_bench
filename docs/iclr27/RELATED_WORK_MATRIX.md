# ICLR 2027 related-work matrix

**Literature cut:** 2026-08-29. Preprints are labeled as such; this matrix does
not infer peer-review status from an arXiv posting.

| Work | Setting and contribution | Closest overlap | Defensible CrashBench distinction | Reviewer risk / required comparison |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [SAFE: Multitask Failure Detection for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09937) (Gu et al., 2025; NeurIPS 2025) | Hidden-state MLP/LSTM failure scores with functional conformal calibration; multi-task/unseen-task tests across OpenVLA, pi0, and pi0-FAST. | Hidden-state risk decoding and runtime alarms. | CrashBench observes option-specific outcomes from exact same states and asks whether/which intervention improves utility, rather than predicting one failure scalar. | Do not claim first hidden-state VLA failure detector. Include SAFE-style temporal/calibration baselines when making sequential claims. |
| [Hide-and-Seek in Trajectories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30834) (Park et al., 2026, preprint) | Coarsely supervised temporal localization of VLA failure signals with conformal runtime monitoring. | The P2/P3 recovery-window problem and trajectory-level failure labels. | CrashBench's positive result is matched-state option selection; its frozen sequential null explicitly shows that statewise separation need not yield reliable first crossing. | Prevent the negative temporal section from being presented as a new state of the art. Compare supervision and timing metrics if reopened. |
| [CheckVLA: Execution-Time Verification with Action-Conditioned World Model for Long-Horizon Mobile Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26789) (Liu et al., 2026, preprint) | A frozen action-conditioned world model verifies open-loop chunks, conformally controls unnecessary first intervention, and rewrites deployable suffixes. | Action-conditioned consequence prediction, sequential verification, calibrated intervention, and repair. | CrashBench uses simulator-observed exact-state option outcomes as training labels and separates Base/Detour/Retreat potential outcomes; it does not learn a rollout world model or suffix rewriter. | This blocks broad “first action-conditioned VLA verification/intervention” language. Sequential evaluation breadth currently favors CheckVLA. |
| [CoWAM: Coordination Contracts for Selective Policy Intervention with WAMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02578) (Liu et al., 2026, preprint) | Conservative preserve/override/abstain selection over shared WAM candidate pools using typed coordination contracts, predicted futures, and calibrated gates. | Selective intervention, candidate-value evidence, nominal preservation, oracle labeling, false/harmful intervention metrics. | CrashBench's clearest distinction is exact serialized-state branching with all realized option outcomes and explicit outcome decomposition in a single-arm VLA safety setting. | Nearest threat. Phase 2 must match strong direct/value baselines, common candidate pools, intervention errors, and latency; “first selective intervention layer” is forbidden. |
| [SafeVLA: Towards Safety Alignment of Vision-Language-Action Model via Constrained Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03480) (Zhang et al., 2025; NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight) | Training-time VLA safety alignment using CMDP/SafeRL and the Safety-CHORES benchmark. | Safety--task tradeoffs and VLA physical-hazard evaluation. | CrashBench leaves the base VLA frozen and studies post-hoc option selection from matched states rather than training a safety-aligned VLA. | Avoid generic “VLA safety benchmark” novelty; emphasize interventional labeling and selector evaluation. |
| [Mechanistic Interpretability for Steering Vision-Language-Action Models](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/haon25a.html) (Häon et al., CoRL 2025) | Identifies interpretable internal directions and steers OpenVLA/pi0 behavior without fine-tuning. | Hidden-state mechanisms and activation steering. | CrashBench reports a scoped negative final-readout steering result and a positive explicit readout-to-controller interface; steering is supporting evidence, not the method headline. | Cite directly and avoid implying that detector directions can never steer VLAs in general. |
| [Counterfactual VLA: Self-Reflective Vision-Language-Action Model with Adaptive Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24426) (Peng et al., CVPR 2026) | Counterfactual reasoning traces revise driving meta-actions and improve trajectory/safety metrics. | “Counterfactual VLA” terminology and option consequences. | CrashBench's counterfactuals are simulator branches from identical full states with realized outcome labels, not generated language reasoning traces; domain is manipulation rather than driving. | Define potential outcomes operationally and avoid novelty claims based only on the word “counterfactual.” |
| [Do No Harm: A Counterfactual Approach to Safe Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11669) (Vaskov, Schwarting, and Baker, L4DC 2024) | Constrains counterfactual harm relative to a default safe policy in safe RL. | Counterfactual safety relative to a baseline action/policy. | CrashBench uses full-information exact-state option outcomes for supervised selective routing, not an RL training constraint or an observational causal estimator. | Situate the potential-outcome language in broader safe decision making; do not claim counterfactual safety itself is new. |
| [Model-Based Runtime Monitoring with Interactive Imitation Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17552) (Liu et al., 2023, preprint) | Predictive monitoring requests human oversight/intervention and supports interactive policy improvement. | Runtime intervention, model-based future prediction, success under a matched intervention budget. | CrashBench evaluates autonomous structured options and exact-state branch outcomes without human takeover or online policy retraining. | Include intervention-rate-matched comparisons and distinguish selector quality from proposal/controller quality. |

## Positioning to use

> Prior VLA safety work detects failure, aligns policies, verifies action
> execution, or selectively replaces actions using predicted futures.
> CrashBench instead uses exact simulator-state branching to observe the
> realized outcomes of each available intervention and asks whether a learned
> selector can recover option value beyond scalar risk under source-paired
> evaluation.

This positioning remains conditional on the phase-2 baseline audit. If direct-Q
or simple geometry matches the outcome router, the contribution must be
reframed as multi-option value learning or an exact-state evaluation protocol,
not outcome decomposition.
