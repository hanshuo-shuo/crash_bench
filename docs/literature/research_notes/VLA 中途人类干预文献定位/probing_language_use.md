# Probing whether VLAs read and use the instruction internally (2023 – October 2026)

Verification convention: every arXiv ID below was checked on 2026-10-31 against its arXiv abstract/HTML page or the arXiv export API (title, authors, first-submission date). Numbers come from the fetched abstract or HTML pages unless marked **[search-summary only]** (seen only in a search-engine summary, not re-read on the paper) or **[unverified]**. Overlap ratings compare each work with our finding: pi0.5 does not act on mid-task instruction changes, yet swapping the instruction at a fixed observation changes the first action chunk 2.4–9.4x more than seed resampling does (empty instruction 1.4–2.9x), and that sensitivity either persists or fades over the episode depending on task family.

## Q1. Mechanistic interpretability of VLAs: what do attention, probing, logit-lens and steering studies find about language, including over time in an episode?

### Takeaway
By late 2026 a mechanistic literature on pi0/pi0.5/OpenVLA exists. Its consistent result is that the instruction is *encoded* (linear probes >99%) but is *weighted below vision* whenever the scene identifies the goal (Grant et al., arXiv 2603.19233, on pi0.5/LIBERO). No study we found measures how instruction sensitivity evolves across an episode for pi0.5. There are only fragments: an OpenVLA-OFT early/middle versus late prompt-nulling window result, and "most sensitive at trajectory initiation" for GR00T feature ablations.

### Cited Findings
- **Häon, Stocking, Chuang, Tomlin (UC Berkeley), "Mechanistic interpretability for steering vision-language-action models", CoRL 2025 (PMLR 305), arXiv 2509.00328 (2025-08-30).** Models: pi0, OpenVLA. LIBERO and UR5. Method: projects FFN value vectors onto the token-embedding basis (a logit-lens-style readout) to find sparse semantic directions (speed, direction), then steers activations in real time. Findings: "less than 25% of FFN neurons" are rewired for action prediction. Semantic value-vector patterns survive VLA training "at similar rates", and task fine-tuning changes mainly action tokens; DROID instruction tokens appear only about 1.2x more often after fine-tuning. "Fast" versus "slow" clusters give 27.73% larger end-effector displacement on average (up to 148.54%, p<0.001). On the robot, "modifying the prompt had weaker impact than our steering intervention." The paper has no instruction-swap or within-episode analysis. **Overlap: low–medium.** It shows language-like internal directions can drive actions more strongly than the prompt itself, a hint that the prompt channel under-drives behavior, but it does not test instruction changes. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00328); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00328v1); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/haon25a.html)
- **Grant, Zhao, Wang (Case Western Reserve Univ.), "Not All Features Are Created Equal: A Mechanistic Study of Vision-Language-Action Models", NeurIPS 2026 poster and ICLR 2026 Multimodal Intelligence Workshop oral, arXiv 2603.19233 (v1 2026-03-19, v5 2026-10-26).** Models: pi0.5 (main), OpenVLA-OFT, X-VLA, SmolVLA, GR00T and one more (six VLAs, over 420k rollouts). Benchmarks: LIBERO, MetaWorld, SimplerEnv, ALOHA, plus a physical UR10e. Methods: activation injection into the action expert, over 520 SAEs, linear probes, VLM-pathway edits at instruction positions, and counterfactual prompts (negation, null, swap, and a "temporal switch" that changes the prompt mid-episode).

It differs from us in using episode-start prompts and representation-level probes. It has no seed-normalized action-chunk sensitivity and no persists/fades profile, and we found no reported result for its temporal-switch condition. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19233); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19233)
- **Shi et al., "VLA-Trace: Diagnosing Vision-Language-Action Models through Representation and Behavior Tracing", arXiv 2605.30117 (v1 2026-05-28, v3 2026-10-18).** Models: pi0.5, OpenVLA. Methods: cross-modal and checkpoint-drift CKA, attention knockout between vision and language spans (for pi0.5 during prefill), and rollout probes of grounding, shortcut dependence and semantic following. - **Zhou et al. (Tsinghua; LingYu Robotics), "Gaze Prompts: Temporally Dense Human Attention for VLA Fine-Tuning", arXiv 2609.34550 (2026-09-28).** Model: pi0. ### Inferences
- The "language token attention collapse" pattern the brief asks about appears in three forms. Late layers of pi0 collapse onto sink/register tokens (Gaze Prompts). BOS sinks dominate text attention (VLA-Trace, search-summary only). Attention maps barely change under an instruction swap (JSD 0.047, Gaze Prompts). We found no paper that names "language token attention collapse" as such.
- On episode time, the closest evidence to our "fades" family is the OFT window-nulling result in Grant et al. Prompt information is necessary early and in the middle; late nulling spares some tasks. This is consistent with language mattering less late in an episode, but it is a different model and an activation intervention, not an instruction swap.
- Attention and probe evidence warns that "encoded" does not imply "used" (VLA-Trace, Read-Best/Steer-Best, the driving depth probe). Our first-chunk sensitivity is a stronger, output-level reading test than a linear probe. The novel part of our result is that output-level sensitivity still does not translate into closed-loop following.

### Gaps
- We did not find the result for Grant et al.'s "temporal switch" condition in the parts of the paper we read. A search-engine summary claimed it "only restores a goal the policy already pursues and does not test overriding a competing instruction" **[unverified]**.
- No paper applies a per-layer vocabulary logit lens to instruction tokens in a manipulation VLA. Häon et al.'s projection onto the token basis is the closest; the only per-layer lens study is the driving one (2608.07361).
- No per-timestep curve of language attention or instruction sensitivity for pi0/pi0.5 was found.
- Several affiliations were not printed on the abstract pages (VLA-Trace, COAST, Mitra et al.); the group attributions above are inferred from author names only where stated.
- A claim that "masking all language tokens yields only a marginal success drop; attention concentrates on the first language token across blocks and sampling steps" appeared in a search summary, but we could not identify the source paper. It is excluded from the findings.

## Q2. Counterfactual-instruction and action-sensitivity probes; classifier-free guidance and contrastive decoding on language

### Takeaway
Counterfactual-instruction tests are now standard: blank, wrong, swapped, negated and paraphrased prompts. Almost all of them score **task success or grounding**, not the **change in predicted actions relative to sampling noise**. We found no published probe that normalizes instruction-swap action change by seed resampling, which is our design. The nearest normalizations use camera blanking as the reference scale (driving) or per-replan disagreement across paraphrases. CFG-style language guidance for VLAs exists and works on pi0.5 (CAG). It contrasts a vision-only branch with the language-conditioned branch, so its conditional-minus-unconditional term is essentially our empty-instruction contrast used as a control knob.

### Cited Findings

It perturbs seven dimensions. Models "are largely insensitive to language variations," and further experiments reveal "models tend to ignore language instructions completely." Blank-instruction detail **[search-summary only]**: OpenVLA-OFT on LIBERO-Object is largely unchanged with a blank instruction, with a significant drop only on LIBERO-Long; the authors say the model "degenerates" into a vision-action model. **Overlap: medium.** It is the main "ignores language" claim, and our probe refines it: pi0.5's output does read the instruction. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13626); [CVPR 2026 PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Fei_LIBERO-Plus_A_Progressive_Robustness_Benchmark_for_Visual-Language-Action_Models_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Probe-design positioning:** our ratio, instruction-swap action change over seed-resampling action change at a fixed observation, appears new. It sits between success-based reliance (language share, 2610.19438), representation probes (2603.19233), KV-cache swaps (2609.39971), attention-map divergence (2609.34550) and normalized counterfactual sensitivity in driving (2610.30953). Citing 2610.30953 for the normalization idea and 2610.19438 for the fluent-wrong-instruction-versus-blank caveat would pre-empt reviewer questions. Our swap condition is a fluent, in-distribution instruction, so it already avoids the blank-prompt confound; the empty-instruction ratio of 1.4–2.9x plays the role of their blank control.
- The CAG formula makes explicit that ‖π_cond − π_uncond‖ (our empty-instruction contrast) is the quantity CFG amplifies. A small but nonzero contrast plus a large swap contrast suggests CFG could raise the language term at switch time. This is untested for mid-task switches, and any such run is a new intervention requiring user approval under the project rules.
- 2610.21251's result that corrections (12.8%) barely beat inverted corrections (11.8%) is a behavioral analogue of "the content of a mid-episode instruction is not acted on" in pi0-family and other VLAs.

### Gaps
- No study found reports per-timestep instruction-swap sensitivity for any VLA.
- No study found uses sampling-noise (seed) variance as the reference scale for language sensitivity in flow-matching VLAs. A search-engine summary explicitly found none.
- The ACG remark on language-CFG instability and the LIBERO-Plus blank-instruction numbers were not re-read on the papers.

## Q3. Explanations for language neglect: vision dominance, one-task-per-scene data, knowledge insulation, frozen versus fine-tuned backbone, forgetting

### Takeaway
The explanations converge on one data-level cause and one training-level cause. The data cause: in goal-driven robot datasets the instruction is predictable from the scene, so conditional mutual information between instruction and action collapses ("Information Collapse", LangForce; "modality imbalance", BayesVLA; "vision overrides language", LIBERO-CF). The training cause: fine-tuning on narrow robot data erodes VLM representations ("instruction blindness"; forgetting), which knowledge insulation in pi0.5 only partially fixes. Physical Intelligence itself says pi0.5+KI language following is "still far from perfect."

### Cited Findings
- **Driess, Springenberg, Ichter, Yu, Li-Bell, Pertsch, Ren, Walke, Vuong, Shi, Levine (Physical Intelligence), "Knowledge Insulating VLA Models: Train Fast, Run Fast, Generalize Better", arXiv 2505.23705 (2025-05-29).**
  - Mechanism: the PaliGemma backbone is trained on FAST-token next-token prediction plus VLM data, and a separate flow-matching action expert reads backbone keys and values under a stop-gradient. Information flows one way, from VLM to expert.
  - "Allowing gradients from the action expert to the backbone (joint-training or π0) harms language following" (Fig. 4b). pi0 "struggles with following language instructions" (Fig. 5), and in Fig. 2 pi0 "ignores the command and grasps a piece of trash instead."
  - Removing VLM co-training hurts language following most for joint training (Fig. 6a).
  - Limitations: language following is improved but "still far from perfect", likely because training-data correlations make the model ignore instructions.
  - Exact rates appear only in figures.

  **Overlap: medium.** It is the official explanation for language neglect in the pi0 family and the reason pi0.5 is expected to read language. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23705); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23705)
- **openpi README (Physical Intelligence):** pi0.5 is "an upgraded version of π0 … trained with knowledge insulation". pi05_droid is "fine-tuned on the DROID dataset with knowledge insulation". The pi05_libero entry ("fine-tuned for the LIBERO benchmark") does not state whether LIBERO fine-tuning used KI. — [GitHub](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi)
- **Physical Intelligence, "π0.5: a VLA Model with Open-World Generalization", arXiv 2504.16054 (2025-04-22).** It defines a "language following rate" (how often the robot selects the object named in the command). Language following improves with the number of training locations. Removing multi-environment or cross-embodiment data "significantly degrades language following," and removing web data hurts out-of-distribution objects (Figs. 9 and 11; numbers only in the figures). **Overlap: low–medium.** It establishes that PI measures language following only at episode start. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16054); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16054)
- **Lian, Yu, Lin, Yang, Shen, Wu et al., "LangForce: Bayesian Decomposition of VLA Models via Latent Action Queries", ICML 2026, arXiv 2601.15197 (2026-01-21; an earlier version was reportedly titled BayesianVLA **[search-summary only]**).** In goal-driven datasets "language instructions are highly predictable from visual observations alone, causing the conditional mutual information between instructions and actions to vanish ('Information Collapse')," so "models degenerate into vision-only policies that ignore language constraints." The fix trains a vision-only prior p(a|v) and a posterior π(a|v,ℓ) and maximizes conditional pointwise mutual information. It reports +11.3% OOD on SimplerEnv **[search-summary only]**. **Overlap: medium.** Information collapse predicts that once a task is under way, the scene "explains" the original instruction, which is a plausible mechanism for ignoring mid-task changes. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15197); [ICML poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/65457)
- **Xu et al., "Seeing to Act, Prompting to Specify: A Bayesian Factorization of VLA Policy" (BayesVLA), arXiv 2512.11218 (2025-12-12).** It names "modality imbalance, where language diversity is much lower than visual and action diversity," which "biases the model toward visual shortcuts and language forgetting." It splits the policy into a visual-action prior and a language likelihood, and adds explicit pre-contact and post-contact phases. **Overlap: medium.** Its phase split matches our observation that language influence can depend on episode phase. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11218)
- **Zhang and Bisk, "Flatness Preserves Instruction Following in VLA Models", arXiv 2606.23641 (2026-06-22).** Fine-tuning on limited robot data creates sharp minima and "instruction blindness" ("ignore language instructions in favor of visual shortcuts"). Sharpness-aware minimization improves instruction following by "over 60%" and is "complementary to existing guidance techniques." **Overlap: low–medium.** — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23641)
- **Xing, Luo, Xie, Gao, Shen, Song, "Shortcut Learning in Generalist Robot Policies: The Role of Dataset Diversity and Fragmentation", CoRL 2025, arXiv 2508.06426.** Shortcut learning is driven by limited within-sub-dataset diversity and large between-sub-dataset disparities. Studied on Diffusion Policy, MiniVLA and pi0. **Overlap: low–medium.** — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06426)
- **Catastrophic forgetting of VLM abilities:**
  - Hancock, Wu, Zha, Russakovsky, Majumdar (Princeton), "Actions as Language: Fine-Tuning VLMs into VLAs Without Catastrophic Forgetting" (VLM2VLA), arXiv 2509.22195 (2025-09-26). Action fine-tuning "diminishes the VLM's foundational reasoning and multimodal understanding, hindering … instruction following." Representing actions as language plus LoRA preserves VQA ability and multilingual instruction following. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22195)
  - Yang et al., "InstructVLA", arXiv 2507.17520. VLAs "suffer catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained vision-language capabilities." — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17520)
  - Xu et al., "Semantic Anchoring for Robotic Action Representations", CoRL 2026, arXiv 2607.13597. "Systematic probing confirms that this [semantic] structure erodes during finetuning," and its quality tracks success and OOD generalization. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13597)

  **Overlap: low.**
- **One instruction per episode in training (2610.21251).** "Pairing each training episode with a single instruction limits How-level instruction following." Segment-level instructions make actions responsive but do not extend to demanding or OOD How-level instructions. **Overlap: medium–high.** This is the most direct data explanation for ignoring mid-task instruction changes. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21251)
- **Scene-identifies-goal account (2603.19233)** and **instruction-retrieves-trajectory account (2609.39971)**: see Q1 and Q2. Both give mechanistic stories in which pi0.5 reads the instruction to pick a stored motor program, and vision or absolute-coordinate binding then dominates execution. — [2603.19233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19233); [2609.39971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39971)

### Inferences
- For mid-task changes, three explanations predict our pattern of "reads but doesn't act; weaker late":
  1. Information collapse or modality imbalance: progress in the scene makes the original task identifiable from vision alone (LangForce, BayesVLA, Grant et al.'s "consults it only when the scene leaves the goal open").
  2. One-instruction-per-episode supervision: there are no training examples in which the instruction changes while the scene state stays committed (2610.21251).
  3. Instruction-as-trajectory-retrieval with visual closed-loop correction (2609.39971): a one-step change in the first chunk can be undone by the next replans, because the visual state keeps cueing the source trajectory family. This is a hypothesis for why a 2.4–9.4x first-chunk change does not produce a behavioral switch.
- Knowledge insulation explains why pi0.5 *reads* language better than pi0. It does not address the absence of mid-task instruction changes in fine-tuning data. Whether pi05_libero fine-tuning kept KI is not stated in the README.

### Gaps
- We found no study that tests whether KI or co-training changes *mid-task* instruction following.
- We found no paper that measures frozen-backbone versus fine-tuned-backbone instruction sensitivity at matched observations over an episode.
- LangForce's numerical gains were seen only in a search summary.

## Q4. Does a "policy understands the instruction but does not execute it" (perception–action / knowing–doing gap) result already exist, and how does ours differ?

### Takeaway
Yes, the general "encoded but not acted on" framing exists in several forms by October 2026. The closest manipulation versions are on pi0.5/LIBERO: Grant et al. 2603.19233 (instruction encoded and probe-decodable but under-weighted versus vision; negation ignored) and Chen et al. 2609.39971 (language not ignored but bound to familiar trajectories). There are explicit "binding gap" papers in driving VLAs (2610.28184, 2610.30953) and the intention–execution gap of INT-ACT (2506.09930). We found no prior work showing it for **mid-task instruction changes**, with an **output-level, seed-normalized** reading measure, and with a **temporal profile** (persists versus fades).

### Cited Findings
- **Fang, Zhang, Tong, Feng (NYU AI4CE), "From Intention to Execution: Probing the Generalization Boundaries of VLA Models" (INT-ACT), arXiv 2506.09930 (2025-06-11; under review).** A 50-task probing suite in 10 subcategories. "VLM backbones endow VLAs with robust perceptual understanding and high level planning, which we refer to as good intentions, [but] this does not reliably translate into precise motor execution." "Finetuning on action data can erode the original VLM's generalist reasoning abilities." **Overlap: medium.** It coins the intention–execution gap, but about OOD motor execution, not about a changed instruction being ignored. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09930); [project](https://ai4ce.github.io/INT-ACT/)
- **Cercola, Luo, Formentin, Pavone, "Perceives but Doesn't Brake: A Perception-Action Binding Gap in Reasoning Driving VLAs", arXiv 2610.28184 (2026-10-20).** Model: Alpamayo-1.5.
  - "Hazard information is available inside the model but exerts insufficient control over trajectory generation." Plans are dominated by ego-speed history and respond weakly to hazards or to the model's own reasoning.
  - "The trajectory generator is capable of braking but sometimes fails to do so."
  - A hazard-conditioned energy injected into the frozen flow-matching sampler cuts footprint intrusion from 58.1% to 14.5%.

  **Overlap: high conceptually, low in domain.** It is the clearest "encoded but not acted on" paper and uses inference-time guidance to bind perception to action, but it concerns visual hazards in driving rather than instructions. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28184v1)
- **Manalu et al., arXiv 2610.30953 (driving reasoning–action coupling).** The stated reasoning is "weakly coupled" to the trajectory. **Overlap: medium.** — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30953)
- **Wu, Li, Hermans, Ramos, Bajcsy, Pérez-D'Arpino, "Do What You Say: Steering VLA Models via Runtime Reasoning-Action Alignment Verification", arXiv 2510.16281 (2025-10-18).** "Even with a correct textual plan, the generated actions can still miss the intended outcomes," which the authors call a lack of embodied-CoT faithfulness. The fix samples action candidates, simulates them and has a VLM pick the one consistent with the plan, giving up to 15% gain on a LIBERO-100 extension. **Overlap: medium.** It is a say–do gap for the model's own plan, fixed by an outside verifier. That resembles our project's gate idea, though not instruction changes. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16281)
- **Xu and Wu, "The Human–VLA Communication Gap: What Humans Leave Implicit and What Policies Cannot Recover", arXiv 2610.17746 (2026-10-13).** pi0.5, with consistent trends on pi0-FAST and Cosmos3-Edge. "A robot may know how to perform a task yet fail when a human asks for it naturally." Even after the information gap is closed, "execution remains sensitive to command granularity and invocation state." **Overlap: medium.** It is a pi0.5 execution gap tied to when and how a command is issued; it is not a mid-task switch probe. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17746); [project](https://commgap.github.io/)
- **COAST (2605.17144).** VLAs "retain substantial task-relevant knowledge in their latent representations," while the action expert's decoding is the bottleneck. **Overlap: medium.** — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17144)
- **Li et al., "SwitchVLA: Execution-Aware Task Switching for VLA Models", arXiv 2506.03574 (2025-06-04).** VLAs "typically assume static task intent, failing to respond when new instructions arrive during ongoing execution." The fix trains with execution-state-conditioned, contact-phase-segmented demonstrations. **Overlap: medium.** It reports the behavioral failure and a training fix, with no internal reading probe. Mid-task switching is likely covered in more depth by the parallel intervention-literature notes. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03574)
- **Grant et al. (2603.19233), Chen et al. (2609.39971), Park et al. (2610.21251), driving depth probe (2608.07361):** see Q1–Q3.

- **What our result adds:**
  1. Reading is shown at the *action-output* level, not only in representations: the first-chunk change is 2.4–9.4x the seed variance.
  2. The gap is between that one-step sensitivity and *closed-loop* adoption of a *mid-task* instruction.
  3. Sensitivity has a task-family-dependent time course (persists versus fades), which no paper reports.

  Phrasings to avoid: "first to show VLAs encode instructions they don't follow" (Grant et al. and Chen et al. pre-empt this), and "VLAs ignore language" (contradicted by our own probe and by 2609.39971).
- **Methods to cite, or adopt with approval:**
  - Fluent wrong-instruction reliance (2610.19438) as the success-level companion to our swap ratio.
  - Prefix-KV swap (2609.39971) and VLM-pathway edit or activation injection (2603.19233) as causal tests of where a mid-task instruction is lost.
  - Linear probes for the current instruction per timestep (2603.19233 style) to separate "encoded late" from "used late."
  - CAG / CFG on language (2602.17659) as a training-free amplifier.
  - Camera-blanking normalization (2610.30953) as an alternative reference scale.
  - Attention-map JSD under instruction swap (2609.34550) as a cheap attention-level companion.

  Any new pi0.5 run is a new execution needing user go-ahead under project rules.

### Gaps
- We could not confirm whether any paper ran a mid-episode instruction switch on pi0.5 with an internal probe; Grant et al.'s temporal-switch results were not located.
- We found no use of the phrase "knowing–doing gap" for VLAs. The term comes from LLM-agent work, which we did not search because it is outside scope.
- A Case Western paper on "Imagining Recovery" (CoRe), said in a search summary to handle mid-episode instruction changes for frozen VLAs, could not be matched to an arXiv ID **[unverified]**.
- Venues for several 2026 preprints (2602.17659, 2609.39971, 2610.19438, 2610.21251, 2610.28184) are not listed on arXiv; treat them as preprints.
