# Human-in-the-loop intervention learning and physical-perturbation robustness of visuomotor policies / VLAs (2019 – Oct 2026)

Verification method (2026-10-31): every arXiv ID below was checked against the arXiv export API (title, authors, first-submission date, author comment). For most papers, claims about what was perturbed and the numbers come from keyword searches of the arXiv HTML full text (arxiv.org/html or ar5iv). Searched terms: perturb, disturb, displac, push, moved, human interfer, recover, inject, mid-, interject. Venues are given only when the arXiv author comment states them. Other venues are marked "(venue not confirmed)". When a keyword search found nothing, that means "not found in the paper text", not "proven absent"; project-page videos were not watched.

Our setting, for reference: a person speaks to the robot (stop / return / cancel / switch) or physically disturbs it mid-task (target moved 6 cm, grasped object taken out and put on the table, arm pushed 10 cm, receptacle moved 6 cm). The instruction stays the same in the physical case. We evaluate the frozen policy's response against a same-step control.

## Q1. What does "human-in-the-loop / intervention" mean in robot learning, what role does the human play, and does any of this work evaluate the policy's response to a human acting mid-task?

### Takeaway
In the interactive imitation learning / HIL-RL lineage (HG-DAgger → Sirius → RLIF → HIL-SERL → π*0.6 RECAP → 2026 VLA HIL post-training), an "intervention" almost always means one of two things. Either a human (or a gating rule) takes over control through teleoperation (steering wheel, SpaceMouse, phone, VR, kinesthetic handle) to supply corrective actions, or the human rewinds the robot. The person shares the robot's goal and acts to fix its mistake. The intervention data is a training signal, and evaluation measures the post-trained autonomous policy, not how a frozen policy reacts to a person acting on it. Two smaller lines come closer to our setting. In the language-correction line (Interactive Language, OLAF, YAY Robot, RT-H, Hi Robot), speech mid-task is an input the policy must respond to. In the physical-HRI line (Bajcsy/Losey/Dragan), a human push is treated as intentional information about the objective. Neither line tests "a frozen VLA ignores speech but absorbs pushes".

### Cited Findings
**A. Interactive imitation learning: the human takes over via teleop or gating (training signal)**
- **HG-DAgger: Interactive Imitation Learning with Human Experts.** Authors: Kelly, Sidrane, Driggs-Campbell, Kochenderfer (Stanford). arXiv 1810.02890, Oct 2018 (commonly cited as ICRA 2019; venue not confirmed in arXiv metadata). Setting: simulated and real autonomous driving. Human role: the expert takes full control when they judge it necessary ("human-gated"), which avoids DAgger's problem of labeling actions without being in control. The method also learns a safety threshold on an uncertainty-based risk metric. Result: better than DAgger and BC. Overlap: **low**. The human is a teleop expert generating labels, and the policy is never evaluated on how it responds to the human. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02890)
- **EnsembleDAgger: A Bayesian Approach to Safe Imitation Learning.** Authors: Menda, Driggs-Campbell, Kochenderfer. IROS 2019; arXiv 1807.08364. Setting: inverted pendulum and MuJoCo HalfCheetah. Human role: none. The variance of a novice ensemble decides when the (simulated) expert acts ("robot-gated"). Overlap: **low**. The "intervention" is a learned switching rule, not a human. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08364)
- **ThriftyDAgger: Budget-Aware Novelty and Risk Gating for Interactive Imitation Learning.** Authors: Hoque, Balakrishna, Novoseller, Wilcox, Brown et al. CoRL 2021 (oral); arXiv 2109.08273. Human role: the robot asks a human to take over at novel or risky states, within a budget. Results: physical cable routing; 100% success at execution time in both sim and physical tasks. In a user study (N=10, each user supervising 3 robots), human and robot performance were 58% and 80% higher than the next-best method. Overlap: **low**. The interventions are robot-requested teleop takeovers. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08273)
- **LazyDAgger** (Hoque et al., CASE 2021, arXiv 2104.00053) reduces how often control switches between robot and human. **Fleet-DAgger** (Hoque et al., CoRL 2022 oral, arXiv 2206.14349) has remote humans serving robot fleets: 100 simulated robots, plus 4 ABB YuMi arms with 2 remote humans; up to 8.8x higher "return on human effort". Its motivation cites industrial fleets (Amazon, Nimble, Plus One, Waymo, Zoox) that "query remote human teleoperators when robots are at risk". Overlap: **low**. These works define "intervention" as remote teleop. — [LazyDAgger](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00053); [Fleet-DAgger](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14349)
- **Human-in-the-Loop Imitation Learning using Remote Teleoperation (IWR, Intervention Weighted Regression).** Authors: Mandlekar, Xu, Martín-Martín, Zhu, Fei-Fei et al. arXiv 2012.06733, Dec 2020 (venue not confirmed). Setting: 6-DoF manipulation, a threading task and coffee-pod insertion. Human role: remote operators watch the policy and "take over control when they encounter failures" at bottleneck regions; intervention samples are up-weighted. Result: better than non-interventional data of equal size and than other intervention-learning baselines. Overlap: **low**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06733)
- **Robot Learning on the Job: Human-in-the-Loop Autonomy and Learning During Deployment (Sirius).** Authors: Liu, Nasiriany, Zhang, Bao, Zhu (UT Austin RPL). RSS 2023; arXiv 2211.08416. Human role: the operator watches deployment and takes over with a SpaceMouse. Samples are re-weighted by an approximation of human trust (weighted BC). Results: +8% success over the state of the art in sim and +27% on real hardware; convergence twice as fast; 85% smaller memory. Overlap: **low**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08416)
- **Sirius-Fleet: Multi-Task Interactive Robot Fleet Learning with Visual World Models.** Authors: Liu et al. CoRL 2024; arXiv 2410.22689. Anomaly predictors built on a visual world model decide when to ask humans to correct; evaluated on RoboCasa and Mutex. Overlap: **low**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22689)
- **BC-Z: Zero-Shot Task Generalization with Robotic Imitation Learning.** Authors: Jang, Irpan, Khansari, Kappler, Ebert et al. CoRL 2021; arXiv 2202.02005. Data was collected partly by shared autonomy: the operator "coaches the learned policy by fixing its mistakes" (HG-DAgger style; there is an HG-DAgger ablation appendix). Totals: 25,877 demos, 125 hours, 12 robots, 7 operators. Full-text search found no mid-episode perturbation evaluation. Overlap: **low**. It shows that in a 2021 language-conditioned policy, "intervention" already meant teleop coaching for data. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02005)

**B. HIL reinforcement learning: the intervention as reward or correction**
- **RLIF: Interactive Imitation Learning as Reinforcement Learning.** Authors: Luo, Dong, Zhai, Ma, Levine. ICLR 2024; arXiv 2311.12996. Human role: an intervening (possibly suboptimal) expert. The *intervention signal itself is used as the reward* in off-policy RL. Strongly outperforms DAgger-like methods, especially with suboptimal experts; includes real-world vision-based manipulation. Overlap: **low**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12996)
- **Precise and Dexterous Robotic Manipulation via Human-in-the-Loop RL (HIL-SERL).** Authors: Luo, Xu, Wu, Levine. arXiv 2410.21845, Oct 2024 (venue not confirmed). Human role: "a human can intervene the robot by using a SpaceMouse, which will then take over the control of the robot from the RL policy". Interventions are frequent early on and taper off. Results: near-perfect success after 1–2.5 h of training; on average 2x success and 1.8x faster than the baselines. *Physical robustness, qualitative only:* Fig. 6 shows "RAM insertion under external perturbations, such as a moving motherboard", retrying after "the grippers are forced open", "objects being intentionally dropped by a human from the gripper", a belt "repositioned dynamically during the assembly", and USB regrasping. The authors also say a relative end-effector frame lets the policy succeed when the object "is perturbed in the middle of the episode". Overlap: **medium** for our physical half. These are qualitative recoveries from human physical disturbance, but in single-task RL policies, not a language-conditioned VLA, with no counts and no control. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21845)
- **ConRFT: A Reinforced Fine-tuning Method for VLA Models via Consistency Policy.** Authors: Chen et al. arXiv 2502.05450, Feb 2025. A VLA is fine-tuned offline (BC + Q-learning) and then online "with human interventions to ensure safe exploration". Results: 96.3% average success on 8 real tasks after 45–90 min, +144% success, 1.9x shorter episodes. Overlap: **low**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05450)
- **π*0.6: a VLA That Learns From Experience (RECAP).** Authors: Physical Intelligence (Amin, Aniceto, Balakrishna, Black et al.). arXiv 2511.14759, Nov 2025. Human role: "expert teleoperated interventions provided during autonomous execution" plus sparse outcome rewards. The authors say it is a form of "human-gated DAgger". Training is advantage-conditioned offline RL. Tasks: laundry in real homes, box assembly, espresso. On the hardest tasks it "more than doubles task throughput and roughly halves the task failure rate". Overlap: **low**. In PI's own usage, "intervention" means a teleop correction used as training data. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14759)

**C. Corrections given physically on the robot, or by rewinding**
- **Compliant Residual DAgger (CR-DAgger).** Authors: Xu, Hou, Xin, Liu, Song. arXiv 2506.16685, Jun 2025. Human role: a *kinesthetic* compliant interface that "lets humans directly apply force to the robot" to give delta corrections "while the robot policy is still running". A residual policy is learned from these corrections, with force feedback. Base-policy success improves by 64% on book flipping, belt assembly, cable routing and gear insertion. Overlap: **low–medium**. This is the HIL work closest to "a human physically pushes a running policy", but the push is an intended correction recorded for training; how the policy reacts to the push is not evaluated. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16685)
- **RaC: Robot Learning for Long-Horizon Tasks by Scaling Recovery and Correction.** Authors: Hu, Wu, Enock, Li, Kadakia et al. arXiv 2509.07953, Sep 2025. Human role: a teleop intervention "when failure appears imminent". The operator first rewinds the robot to an in-distribution state, then supplies a corrective segment. Tasks: shirt hanging, lid sealing, takeout-box packing, plus a sim assembly task. Uses 10x less data than the prior state of the art, and performance scales linearly with the number of recovery maneuvers. Overlap: **low**. It trains recovery skills but does not test external disturbances. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07953)
- **TRANSIC: Sim-to-Real Policy Transfer by Learning from Online Correction.** Authors: Jiang, Wang, Zhang, Wu, Fei-Fei. CoRL 2024; arXiv 2405.10315. Humans "observe and assist robot policy execution" through intervention and online correction; residual policies close sim-to-real gaps (furniture assembly). Overlap: **low**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10315)

**D. VLA HIL post-training, 2026 (all teleop- or rule-gated; none tests responses to a bystander)**
- **DexHiL** (Han, Chen, Zhao, Xu, Shao et al.; arXiv 2603.09121, Mar 2026). Arm-and-hand teleop interventions for dexterous VLA post-training, with "instantaneous human corrections during execution"; +25% average success over offline-only fine-tuning. Overlap: **low**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09121)
- **ROVE** (Xiao, Tang, Ge, Zhou, Mu et al.; arXiv 2606.17011, Jun 2026). RL for humanoid VLA post-training from imperfect whole-body human interventions (Optimistic Value Estimation). Overlap: **low**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17011)
- **HIL-UMI** (Han, Zeng, Zhang, Zhao, Wang et al.; arXiv 2609.20659, Sep 2026). Robot-free HIL: the policy is queried on a handheld UMI stream, and an Energy Score triggers collection where human and policy actions disagree. Overlap: **low**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20659)
- **SimPT** (Wang, Ye, Zhou, Huang, Zhang et al.; arXiv 2610.29161, 21 Oct 2026). Automated, simulated intervention-based VLA post-training. It compares RECAP, HG-DAgger, DPO+SFT and FlowPRO on 7 RoboTwin tasks. In real-robot HIL post-training, success went from 72% to 96% "across 25" (the abstract is truncated after this). Overlap: **low**. It shows that by late 2026 "intervention" is formalized as automated expert recovery from snapshots. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29161)

**E. Language as the intervention (closest to our language half; another note covers it in depth)**
- **Interactive Language: Talking to Robots in Real Time.** Authors: Lynch, Wahid, Tompson, Ding, Betker et al. (Google). arXiv 2210.06407, Oct 2022 (venue not confirmed). A real-time language-instructable policy: an estimated 93.5% success over 87,000 unique commands, "guided by a human via real-time language" for long-horizon rearrangement. Overlap: **medium**. The policy was trained so that mid-task language changes behavior. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06407)
- **OLAF: Interactive Robot Learning from Verbal Correction.** Authors: Liu, Chen, Zhu, Swaminathan, Kolobov et al. arXiv 2310.17555, Oct 2023. Users correct mistakes verbally ("Stop what you're doing. You should move closer to the cup."). An LLM turns the feedback into a policy update; +20.0% average success. Overlap: **medium**. Its example correction begins with "stop", but the correction is used for learning, not obeyed online by a flat policy. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17555)
- **Yell At Your Robot (YAY Robot).** Authors: Shi, Hu, Zhao, Sharma, Pertsch et al. arXiv 2403.12910, Mar 2024 (venue not confirmed). A hierarchical system (a high-level language policy over a language-conditioned low-level policy): verbal corrections on the fly raise success from 15% to 50%, and training the high level on corrections raises it from 15% to 45%. A flat ACT baseline is compared. Overlap: **medium** for the language half. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12910)
- **RT-H: Action Hierarchies Using Language.** Authors: Belkhale, Ding, Xiao, Sermanet, Vuong et al. arXiv 2403.01823, Mar 2024. Policies are conditioned on "language motions" (for example "move arm forward"). A human can intervene in language mid-execution, and learning from language interventions beats learning from teleop interventions. Overlap: **medium**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01823)
- **Hi Robot: Open-Ended Instruction Following with Hierarchical VLA Models.** Authors: Shi, Ichter, Equi, Ke, Pertsch et al. (Physical Intelligence). ICML 2025; arXiv 2502.19417. A VLM high level is placed over a π0 low level; trained on synthetic "human interjections" ("that's not trash", "leave it alone"). It averages >40% higher instruction accuracy than GPT-4o and beats a *flat VLA* (the same π0 low-level policy without the high level) on table bussing, sandwich making and grocery shopping. Overlap: **medium–high** for the language half. PI itself treats mid-task interjections as something a flat π0 does not handle without a hierarchy and synthetic data; it does not test physical perturbations. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19417)

**F. Physical HRI: a push is information, not noise**
- **Learning Robot Objectives from Physical Human Interaction.** Authors: Bajcsy, Losey, O'Malley, Dragan. CoRL 2017. The paper treats physical contact as "informative about how the robot should be doing its task" rather than as a disturbance to reject; it reports better task performance with less human effort. — [PDF](https://collab.me.vt.edu/pdfs/bajcsy_corl2017.pdf)
- **Physical Interaction as Communication: Learning Robot Objectives Online from Human Corrections.** Authors: Losey, Bajcsy, O'Malley, Dragan. arXiv 2107.02349, Jul 2021. Argues that physical interaction is often intentional (the human intervenes because the robot is doing the task wrong), learns online during the task, and handles noisy corrections. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02349)
- **Learning under Misspecified Objective Spaces.** Authors: Bobu, Bajcsy, Fisac, Dragan. CoRL 2018; arXiv 1810.05157. The robot should first reason about whether a physical correction is relevant before learning from it. Overlap for the whole pHRI line: **medium (conceptual)**. It supplies the counter-framing to "robust to pushes": an intentional push may be a message the robot should act on, not reject. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05157)

### Inferences
- Our "human intervention" (a bystander speaks to or physically disturbs a running frozen policy, and we measure the policy's response) is not what the HIL-learning literature means. There the human is a teleoperating expert who shares the robot's goal, the intervention replaces the policy's actions, and the output is training data. Our paper should say this explicitly, perhaps with a three-part taxonomy: (i) teleop takeover / correction for learning, (ii) language interjection to steer the policy, (iii) physical contact, either as disturbance or as communication.
- None of the HIL-learning papers above evaluates a frozen policy's response to a human acting on it mid-task. The nearest are HIL-SERL's qualitative robustness figure and CR-DAgger's kinesthetic corrections, and in CR-DAgger the push is meant as a correction.
- The pHRI framing yields a sharp extension of our result. Under our protocol, rejecting a push while the instruction is unchanged is the right behavior. But the same mechanism means a push *intended* as "stop" or "not there" would also be rejected. "Robust to physical disturbance" and "deaf to physical communication" may be the same property seen from two sides.

### Gaps
- I did not check whether any HIL paper from 2025–2026 that I did not find evaluates a frozen VLA under bystander speech or contact. Searches for "stop command interruption VLA" found no such benchmark; the closest are MotorMind's Prompt Shift (Q4) and RESCUE's monitor-triggered stop-and-reset ([arXiv 2610.13516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13516)).
- Venues for HG-DAgger, IWR, HIL-SERL, YAY Robot, RT-H and Interactive Language are not stated in arXiv metadata; HIL-SERL is often cited as Science Robotics 2025 (not verified here).
- I did not open the full texts of DexHiL, ROVE, HIL-UMI or SimPT; their human-role descriptions come from the abstracts.

## Q2. Is perturbation recovery of closed-loop visuomotor policies and VLAs already known? (model papers and demos)

### Takeaway
Yes, qualitatively, and for years. Diffusion Policy (2023) showed real-robot recovery when a person shifted the T block mid-task. HIL-SERL and UMI showed recovery from human pushes, forced gripper opening and moved bases. Physical Intelligence's own π0 and π0.5 pages claim the policies recover "when someone tries to intervene" and "can handle … perturbations". DeepMind claims Gemini Robotics "quickly replans and carries on" when "someone moves an item around". What was missing until 2026 were controlled, counted measurements on open VLAs; Q3 lists the 2026 papers that start to supply them. On its own, the physical half of our result is therefore expected, not novel. Its value is in the calibration: four intervention types, a same-step control, ratios of .93–1.00, on standard LIBERO, for π0.5.

### Cited Findings
- **Diffusion Policy: Visuomotor Policy Learning via Action Diffusion.** Authors: Chi, Xu, Feng, Cousineau, Du et al. RSS 2023, with an extended journal version; arXiv 2303.04137. The real-world Push-T perturbation test was run "in a separate episode" from the main table and used three perturbations:
  1. The camera was blocked by a waving hand for 3 s. The policy "remained on-course".
  2. "We shifted the T block while Diffusion Policy was making fine adjustments … Diffusion policy immediately re-planned to push from the opposite direction".
  3. "We moved the T block while the robot was en route to the end-zone after the first stage's completion. The Diffusion Policy immediately changed course to adjust the T block back to its target and then continued to the end-zone."

  The authors call returning the block after a shift "novel behavior [that] was never demonstrated". For sauce pouring and spreading, the policy "reacted gracefully to external perturbations such as moving the pizza dough by hand". In unperturbed real Push-T (Table 6), the best configuration reaches 0.95 success against a human's 1.00; I parsed the column-to-method mapping from flattened HTML, so treat it as approximate. The perturbation results are qualitative only. Overlap: **high (physical half)**. This is the canonical prior demonstration that a closed-loop visuomotor policy recovers from a person moving the task object mid-episode, but it covers a single task, has no language, no counts and no control. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04137)
- **ACT / ALOHA: Learning Fine-Grained Bimanual Manipulation with Low-Cost Hardware.** Authors: Zhao, Kumar, Levine, Finn. arXiv 2304.13705, Apr 2023 (commonly cited as RSS 2023; not confirmed in metadata). Full-text search found no external-perturbation evaluation. The only related remark is that the Thread Velcro task requires "visual feedback to correct for perturbations with each grasp". Overlap: **low**. Project-page videos were not checked. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705)
- **RT-1** (Brohan et al., arXiv 2212.06817), **RT-2** (Brohan et al., arXiv 2307.15818) and **Octo** (Octo Model Team, arXiv 2405.12213). Full-text search found no mid-episode physical perturbation evaluation; for RT-2 the only hit was the word "displacement" in the action-space definition. Overlap: **low**. — [RT-1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06817); [RT-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15818); [Octo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12213)
- **OpenVLA.** Authors: Kim, Pertsch, Karamcheti, Xiao, Balakrishna et al. arXiv 2406.09246, Jun 2024. Mentions only qualitative "recovering from mistakes such as insecurely grasping objects"; no external perturbations. Overlap: **low**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09246)
- **OpenVLA-OFT: Fine-Tuning VLA Models: Optimizing Speed and Success.** Authors: Kim, Finn, Liang. RSS 2025; arXiv 2502.19645. On ALOHA, the strongest baseline (π0, by context) shows "better reactivity to feedback, often successfully recovering from initial failures". The OFT+ variant adds FiLM, in a section titled "Augmenting OpenVLA-OFT with FiLM for Enhanced Language Grounding", which suggests the authors saw weak language grounding. No external-perturbation test. Overlap: **low** for physical, **medium** as a hint for the language half. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19645)
- **π0: A VLA Flow Model for General Robot Control.** Authors: Black, Brown, Driess, Esmail, Equi et al. (Physical Intelligence). RSS 2025; arXiv 2410.24164. The paper argues that diverse pre-training data gives the model "a repertoire of recoveries and corrections", while training "only on this high-quality data results in a brittle model that does not reliably recover from mistakes". The blog (31 Oct 2024) says that in laundry, "the robot is able to recover when someone tries to intervene in a variety of different ways". Qualitative only. Overlap: **high (claim on the same model family)**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24164); [PI blog](https://www.pi.website/blog/pi0)
- **π0.5: a VLA with Open-World Generalization.** Authors: Physical Intelligence (Black, Brown, Darpinian, Dhabalia et al.). arXiv 2504.16054, Apr 2025. Full-text search of the paper found no perturbation keywords. The blog (22 Apr 2025) says: "The policies are reactive, and can handle both variability in the environment and perturbations" and "we test what happens when people interfere with the robot". These are video demos with no numbers, and the page does not discuss changed language commands mid-task. Overlap: **high**. Our physical finding confirms a vendor claim quantitatively, on LIBERO. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16054); [PI blog](https://www.pi.website/blog/pi05)
- **Gemini Robotics: Bringing AI into the Physical World.** Authors: Gemini Robotics Team (Google DeepMind). arXiv 2503.20020, Mar 2025. The paper reports robustness to "visual disturbances, initial condition perturbations, and object shape variations" for an adapted bi-arm Franka model (Sec. D.4.2). The blog (12 Mar 2025) claims the model "continuously monitors its surroundings, detects changes to its environment or instructions" and "if an object slips from its grasp, or someone moves an item around, Gemini Robotics quickly replans and carries on". Overlap: **high (claimed both halves, qualitatively)**. A closed model is claimed to do what we find open models do only on the physical side. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20020); [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/gemini-robotics-brings-ai-into-the-physical-world/)
- **Gemini Robotics 1.5.** arXiv 2510.03342, Oct 2025. The "Thinking VLA" recovers when a water bottle slips: the next thought becomes "pick up the water bottle with the left hand". The safety evaluation includes an "environment attack" with "undesirable disturbances (e.g. moving obstacles)" during a rollout. Overlap: **medium**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03342)
- **UMI: Universal Manipulation Interface.** Authors: Chi, Xu, Pan, Cousineau, Burchfiel et al. arXiv 2402.10329, Feb 2024 (venue not confirmed). Dish washing used "inference time perturbations such as moving robot base, novel objects, different lighting conditions, and adding … more condiments"; "the learned policy is robust against these perturbations" (Fig. 10, video only). Overlap: **medium**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10329)
- **HIL-SERL robustness figure** (arXiv 2410.21845): moving motherboard, grippers forced open, objects dropped out of the gripper by a human, belt repositioned; qualitative (see Q1). Overlap: **medium**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21845)
- **Bidirectional Decoding (BID).** Authors: Liu, Hamid, Xie, Lee, Du et al. arXiv 2408.17355, Aug 2024. Two real dynamic tasks: delivering into "a moving cup held by a human" (Franka) and grasping "a moving cup pulled by a string" (UR5), with 20 episodes per method and setting. BID achieves "2x higher success" than the baselines on dynamic picking. The paper argues that action chunking trades reactivity for consistency. Overlap: **medium**. It counts mid-episode target motion, but on diffusion policies, not VLAs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17355)
- **Real-Time Chunking (RTC).** Authors: Black, Galliker, Levine. NeurIPS 2025; arXiv 2506.07339. "Chunking sacrifices the reactivity of a system to external stimuli". RTC handles latency for π0-style flow VLAs. **FASTER** (arXiv 2603.19199, Mar 2026) models reaction time to external events for π0.5 and X-VLA (table tennis). Overlap: **low–medium**. These works address the mechanism (reaction latency), not recovery outcomes. — [RTC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339); [FASTER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19199)

### Inferences
- The phenomenon "a closed-loop visuomotor policy re-plans when a person moves the object or pushes the robot" has been documented qualitatively since 2023 (Diffusion Policy) and is marketed for π0, π0.5 and Gemini Robotics. Reviewers will likely call the physical half "known". Our framing should present it as a *control condition* that rules out "the policy cannot react mid-task at all" as the explanation for language non-response. It should not be presented as a discovery.
- π0's own rationale (recoveries come from diverse pre-training data) offers a mechanism for why π0.5 absorbs physical disturbances. Physical disturbances move the state toward states that resemble recoveries in the training data. A mid-task "stop" or "return home" utterance has no analogue in demonstration data, where the instruction is fixed per episode. This is our inference, not a claim from the papers.

### Gaps
- PI and DeepMind give no counts for their perturbation demos; I did not watch the videos, so I cannot say which perturbation types (object moved, arm pushed, object taken out of the gripper) they show.
- ACT/ALOHA and Mobile ALOHA project pages may show perturbation videos; not checked.
- None of these model papers reports a same-step control or a ratio comparable to ours.

## Q3. Systematic VLA perturbation benchmarks: do any perturb mid-episode, and with what numbers?

### Takeaway
The large 2024–2025 VLA robustness benchmarks (LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-PRO, COLOSSEUM, VLATest, Eva-VLA, INT-ACT) perturb only the *initial* scene, robot state, camera or instruction. They report severe brittleness, including π0 dropping from 94.2% to 6.6% under initial robot-state perturbation in LIBERO-Plus. Mid-episode perturbation of VLAs is measured only in 2026 papers, mostly as stress tests for correction add-ons:
- ReViP: Object Drop, Distractor Swap and Relayout on LIBERO.
- VLA-Corrector (stage-aware): frozen π0.5 on LIBERO with 10 cm target and goal shifts, 93.7% clean → 78.3% / 77.6%.
- VLA-Corrector (adaptive horizon): π0.5 on a real PiPER arm, disturbance group at 40.0%.
- MotorMind: π0.5 Scene Shift at 70%.
- CycleVLA, VLA-Feedback, FRBench, RoboStress, MAIL-Bench.

The evidence is mixed. π0.5 largely absorbs mid-episode target and goal shifts (consistent with us), while OFT and π0 often fail after a forced object drop (ReViP), which is a caution for generalizing our π0.5 physical result to OFT. I found no standard benchmark that pushes the arm of a VLA mid-episode.

- **VLA-Corrector: Stage-Aware Observable State Understanding for Prompt-Based Closed-Loop Recovery of VLA Policies.** Authors: Song, Qian, Feng, Song. arXiv 2609.06508, 6 Sep 2026. Uses the frozen `pi05_libero` π0.5 on standard LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus. The perturbations are "execution deviations": action noise (σ = 0.1), target-object XY shift of 0.1 m, goal-region XY shift of 0.1 m, and all combined. Over 1,000 episodes per condition, the No Assistance (plain π0.5) results are: clean 93.7, action noise 85.2, **target-object shift 78.3, goal-region shift 77.6**, combined 70.7 (the text says 70.9). I did not extract the exact trigger step of the shift. Overlap: **high**. This is the same model on the same benchmark with object and receptacle shifts. ### Inferences
- The mid-episode literature on VLAs appeared only in 2026 and is almost entirely method papers that use perturbations to motivate a corrector. No paper sets out to characterize which human physical interventions VLAs absorb, against a matched same-step control. Our four-type, same-step-control design is still a distinct contribution, but its novelty is methodological, not the finding itself.
- The numbers are heterogeneous, and we should cite both directions:
  - Consistent with us: VLA-Corrector (stage-aware) on LIBERO π0.5 keeps about 0.83–0.84 of clean success at 10 cm shifts; MotorMind Scene Shift π0.5 70%; CycleVLA's "naturally absorbs early perturbations".
  - Less rosy: VLA-Corrector (adaptive horizon) real-robot π0.5 at 40% under manual shifts versus 70% plain; ReViP's forced drop beats OFT and π0; LIBERO-Plus initial robot-state perturbation sends π0 to 6.6%.

  Plausible moderators: displacement size (6 cm in ours vs 10 cm), timing (early vs near subtask completion), sim vs real, π0.5 vs π0/OFT, and "taken out and put on the table" vs "dropped".
- Our OFT physical branches (stages 3/5) were not run. ReViP's OFT number (19% average on drop/swap/relayout) is a reason to keep the claim "pi0.5 recovers" model-specific rather than "VLAs recover".

### Gaps
- For VLA-Corrector (2609.06508) I did not extract the exact time step at which target and goal shifts occur. For MotorMind I could not determine trials per task; the percentages over 10 and 5 tasks suggest few trials.
- I could not map ReViP's per-task columns reliably; only the averages are used.
- I found no benchmark that pushes the arm of a VLA mid-episode with counted outcomes. Searches found pushes only in legged-locomotion protocols and in qualitative HIL-SERL and FRBench examples.
- I did not verify whether π0.5 appears in LIBERO-Plus's main table (the extracted rows cover OpenVLA, OFT variants, π0, π0-FAST, Nora and others).

## Q4. Has anyone contrasted physical robustness with language responsiveness in the same policy?

### Takeaway
Only incidentally. The closest is MotorMind (arXiv 2609.38078, Sep 2026). Its Table 5 runs the same OpenPI π0.5 LIBERO checkpoint on "Scene Shift" (object or receiver displaced mid-execution, instruction fixed): **70%**. It runs the same checkpoint on "Prompt Shift" (the instruction is replaced mid-execution after a physical trigger, with no reset): **20%**. GR00T N1.5 scores 10% / 0% and MolmoAct2 50% / 0%. This is the same asymmetry we report. But it is a side table in a VLM-scaffolding paper, with 10 and 5 tasks, novel replacement phrasings, and no framing as a finding about VLAs. LIBERO-Plus contrasts perturbation dimensions in the same policies and finds language ignored. Its physical perturbations, however, are at initialization and hurt badly, so it does not show "physically robust". Gemini Robotics claims both physical replanning and responding to instruction changes, qualitatively. Hi Robot shows a flat π0 handles interjections poorly but tests no physical perturbations. No paper I found frames "robust to physical disturbance, unresponsive to what people say" as its thesis with matched controls.

### Cited Findings
- **MotorMind Table 5** (arXiv 2609.38078). Same table, same π0.5 (`libero_base`):

  | | Dynamic Reasoning | Scene Shift | Dynamic Manipulation | Prompt Shift |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | π0.5 | 0% | **70%** | 0% | **20%** |
  | GR00T N1.5 | 0% | 10% | 0% | 0% |
  | MolmoAct2 | 20% | 50% | 40% | 0% |
  | CaP-X | 30% | 20% | 20% | 60% |

  Prompt Shift tasks: "Change of plan: put the ketchup on the plate instead of in the wooden tray"; insert an intermediate step ("First put the chocolate pudding down on the table, turn on the stove…"); "Avoid the obstacle and continue…"; target replacement. The first three changes happen "during transport after the object has been lifted". For direct VLA policies, the "Direct VLA policies discard queued actions associated with the old instruction and use the revised text". Overlap: **high**. It is a direct, though small and unframed, precedent for our asymmetry on the same model; the replacement instructions are out of distribution, unlike our "switch to another trained instruction" condition. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38078)
- **LIBERO-Plus** (arXiv 2510.13626). Within the same models, it finds "extreme sensitivity" to camera and robot initial state (π0 94.2 → 6.6 under robot-init) but "largely insensitive to language variations … models tend to ignore language instructions completely"; the blank instruction leaves OFT unchanged on LIBERO-Object. Overlap: **high (language)**, **opposite (physical)**. It contrasts modalities, but its physical perturbation is static and harmful. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13626)
- **LIBERO-PRO** (arXiv 2510.03827). Outputs are "unchanged even when given corrupted instructions or even messy tokens", and the models keep grasping when the target is replaced. Overlap: **high (language)**. No mid-episode physical test. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03827)
- **ReViP** (arXiv 2601.16667). Proposes a mechanism: VLAs over-rely on proprioception ("state-dominant modality imbalance"), so they "proceed to the goal" despite visual evidence (46/50). Overlap: **medium**. It offers an explanation for both halves: the policy follows its internal and visual state trajectory, not external signals. Its physical result (failure after a drop) is opposite to ours for π0.5. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16667)
- **Gemini Robotics** (blog, 12 Mar 2025). Claims the model "detects changes to its environment or instructions" and "quickly replans" when "someone moves an item around". Overlap: **high (as a counter-claim)**. A proprietary VLA is claimed to respond to both, with no numbers given. — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/gemini-robotics-brings-ai-into-the-physical-world/)
- **Hi Robot** (arXiv 2502.19417). A flat π0 VLA versus a hierarchical system on mid-task interjections; the hierarchy wins on instruction accuracy and task progress. No physical perturbation arm. Overlap: **medium**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19417)
- **π0.5 blog** (22 Apr 2025). Claims reactivity to "perturbations" and people interfering. The page "does not address changing language commands mid-task" (per my fetch of the page). Overlap: **medium**. The vendor documents the physical half only. — [PI blog](https://www.pi.website/blog/pi05)

### Inferences
- Our "physically robust but language-deaf" framing has one direct numerical precedent (MotorMind: π0.5 70% vs 20%, GR00T 10% vs 0%, MolmoAct2 50% vs 0%). It is small-n, uses novel phrasings and is buried in a scaffold paper, so we should cite it as corroboration, not let it pre-empt us. Our design differs in that it uses a same-step control, standard trained instructions, four physical types, ~235 language trials, and two VLA families.
- Combining LIBERO-Plus/LIBERO-PRO (language ignored) with DP, CycleVLA and VLA-Corrector (closed-loop absorbs object shifts) gives a coherent account. These policies behave like vision-proprioception-to-action controllers whose attractor is the trained task. Physical disturbances move the state, but the attractor is unchanged, so they recover. Language changes alter only an input channel the policy largely ignores mid-episode, so nothing happens. This is a synthesis across papers, not a tested claim.
- The pHRI literature (Q1-F) suggests a stronger framing: the same attractor that makes π0.5 "robust" also makes it unable to receive *physical* communication. A protocol in which the push itself is meant as "stop" would test this.

### Gaps
- No paper reports a matched, same-step comparison of mid-task physical intervention versus mid-task language intervention on the same VLA with controls. I searched arXiv and the web through Oct 2026 with several phrasings; absence is not proof.
- Gemini Robotics' claims of responding to instruction changes are not quantified in the material I read.
- MotorMind trials per task and the exact Prompt Shift triggers for VLA baselines were not fully extracted; treat its 20% and 70% as indicative.
